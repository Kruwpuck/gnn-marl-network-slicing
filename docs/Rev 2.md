# Giving GNN-MARL a Fair Chance: A Legitimate Redesign Plan for a Network-Slicing Thesis with a Null Result

## TL;DR
- Your null result is almost certainly a **task-design artifact, not an architecture verdict**: four of five KPIs saturate because the CMDP constraint never binds and network capacity exceeds calibrated demand. The literature is clear that graph/coordination advantages appear **only when interference coupling and contention are high** — exactly the regime your bug-fix and mild load removed. The single highest-value, fully defensible change is to **recalibrate offered load against a *trained* policy so post-training SLA violation sits near δ, then re-run all 8 algorithms identically.**
- The strongest *honest* contributions available to you are not a same-size KPI win but (a) **zero-shot topology generalization** (train 5 gNB, test 20/50 — MLP/central baselines structurally cannot follow, and this is a recognized standalone contribution at INFOCOM/TNSM/JSAC-tier venues), (b) **training-stability / collapse-rate as a first-class risk metric** (report cell-edge collapse as a binomial proportion with Wilson/Clopper–Pearson CIs and CVaR of worst-seed performance), and (c) **mechanism-level evidence** (GAT attention vs. the true interference matrix). Each is publishable and none is p-hacking.
- The negative result is itself publishable **as a calibration/empirical study** if you preregister the revised regime, report both waves ("in regime A no difference; in regime B the proposed method separates"), and frame wave 1 as a pilot. The line you must not cross: choosing the new regime *because* it favors the GNN. The defensible move is to choose it because it makes the *constraint bind and the task contested* — a property you can define and justify before looking at which algorithm wins.

## Key Findings

1. **The advantage is load/interference-gated.** Multiple peer-reviewed sources show the coordination/graph gain over MLP or independent baselines grows with density/contention and vanishes under light load. In Shen, Shi, Zhang & Letaief's canonical power-control study, the MLP's gap to WMMSE grows from 2% at K=10 links to 12% at K=30 while the GNN stays near-optimal, and non-graph learners' advantage over the baseline vanishes at K=30 because "these models can hardly learn the impact of interference when K is large." In dense-network slicing (Hu et al., ICC 2022) coordinated MARL delivers "more than two-fold increase in resource efficiency," with coordination explicitly justified by "load-coupling inter-cell interference." Your bug-fix pushed good-SIR from 28% to 92–98% and left ~8pp of unused constraint margin — you engineered away the exact regime where graphs help.

2. **5 agents with few neighbors is near the floor of where message passing pays.** GNN-MARL benefits are dominated by scalability and zero-shot transfer, not same-size same-topology performance. Your DQN family showing zero CI separations in 20 comparisons is consistent with the literature, not anomalous.

3. **Zero-shot inductive generalization is a legitimate primary claim.** InforMARL and several networked-MARL papers make cross-topology transfer (not same-size performance) the headline contribution. MLP/central baselines have fixed input dimensions and structurally cannot ingest a larger topology — that asymmetry is the point, and accepted baseline adaptations exist.

4. **Bimodal per-seed collapse should be modeled as a discrete outcome, not smeared into a continuous mean.** With outcomes that are either ~1.78 Mbps or ~0, the mean is meaningless and CIs are enormous by construction. Report collapse *rate* as a binomial proportion; use rliable IQM/probability-of-improvement; increase seeds substantially.

5. **Robustness/lower-collapse-rate is a publishable claim** formalized via worst-case seed, CVaR, or probability-of-failure.

6. **Mechanism-level evidence (GAT attention vs. interference matrix) is accepted** at wireless venues as evidence that coordination is real even when aggregate KPIs saturate — with the caveat that attention interpretability is contested and must be validated causally (ablation), not just correlationally.

7. **Overflow-dominated drops undermine URLLC latency claims** and should be fixed by dimensioning buffers to the delay–bandwidth product so the *deadline* is the binding mechanism.

8. **The legitimacy line is defined by preregistration, transparency, and reporting both regimes.** Choosing a regime to make a constraint bind is design; choosing it because it makes your model win is p-hacking.

9. **Yes, the null result is publishable** — ICBINB (NeurIPS/ICLR), reproducibility tracks, and empirical-study/benchmark formats explicitly welcome "architecture X doesn't help under conditions Y."

## Details

### RQ1 — Load/congestion regime: where do graph methods actually win?

The literature strongly supports your root-cause diagnosis. The clearest quantitative evidence is from GNN wireless power control: Shen, Shi, Zhang & Letaief ("A Graph Neural Network Approach for Scalable Wireless Power Control," IEEE GLOBECOM Workshops 2019; extended as "Graph Neural Networks for Scalable Radio Resource Management," IEEE JSAC 39(1):101–115, Jan. 2021) report (JSAC p.102) that "it was shown in [7] that the performance gap to the WMMSE algorithm is 2% when K = 10 and it becomes 12% when K = 30" for the MLP, while the GNN (IGCNet) holds near-WMMSE performance across all densities. They further observe that for non-graph learners the performance gap to the baseline "vanishes when K=30 ... these models can hardly learn the impact of interference when K is large." The Air-MPNN paper (Wang et al., "Graph Neural Networks for Distributed Power Allocation in Wireless Networks: Aggregation Over-the-Air," IEEE Trans. Wireless Communications, 2023) makes the low-load direction explicit: equal-power allocation "outperforms the WMMSE when the density is low ... because ... it is the optimal policy when the interference between all the D2D pairs is negligible." In other words, when coupling is negligible, the uncoordinated baseline is near-optimal and coordination cannot help — which is precisely your regime after the SIR fix.

In slicing specifically, Hu, Liao, Liu, Wellington & Carle ("Inter-Cell Slicing Resource Partitioning via Coordinated Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning," IEEE ICC 2022, arXiv 2202.12833) report that their coordinated distributed scheme achieves "more than two-fold increase in resource efficiency compared to" the baseline and "provides better slice-aware service performance than the centralized approach with the same limited training time." Coordination is motivated by "load-coupling inter-cell interference," with messages carrying per-slice neighbor load. Note honestly that their headline 2× is DRL-vs-baseline (driven largely by resource "headroom"); the measured resource efficiency is 0.374 for the coordinated scheme versus 0.183 for the traffic-aware baseline (~2.04×), while the *coordination-specific* increment over a centralized MLP is modest (reward 0.775 vs 0.756) and framed around convergence and lower model complexity under limited training. The follow-on (Hu et al., "Inter-Cell Network Slicing with Transfer Learning Empowered MADRL," arXiv 2306.11552) reiterates that "the ever-increasing densification of network deployment leads to complex and non-trivial inter-cell interference, which requires more than inaccurate analytic models to dynamically optimize resource management."

Applicability to your setup: your `delta=0.12` constraint with post-training violation of only 3.5–4.5% means the CMDP never binds — λ converged to 0.58–1.02 and never approached λ_max=100. The task is under-loaded. **Protocol to recalibrate against a trained policy** (this is your #1 action): (i) train the *simplest* baseline (e.g., ippo or central-dqn) to convergence at a candidate `lambda_arrival`; (ii) measure its *post-training* held-out violation; (iii) adjust load upward (or δ downward) until the trained policy's steady-state violation sits *near* δ (the "contested" region), not 8pp below it; (iv) freeze that load and δ *before* running the full 8-algorithm wave. This is the trained-policy analog of the standard practice in slicing papers of choosing an operating point where slices genuinely contend (e.g., contention configurations where "each slice's computational load and inter-VNF bandwidth demands range between 27% and 33% of the total available resources," and 80–92% utilization overload points used in slicing-broker and load-balancing studies). Target: post-training aggregate violation in a 5–12% band with the constraint occasionally active, and utilization high enough that co-slice interference materially couples neighbors.

### RQ2 — Where GNN coordination actually pays off

Problem characteristics that make message passing matter, per the literature:
- **Number of agents / neighbors.** GNN advantages are consistently reported as *scalability* properties (Liu et al., "Graph Neural Network Meets Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning," IEEE Wireless Communications, 2024; and the D2D/power-control line above show the gap growing in K). Five gNB with reuse-1 gives each agent only a handful of coupled neighbors — near the floor where a graph inductive bias can distinguish itself from a well-tuned MLP. Moving to 10–20+ gNB is the single largest lever for same-metric separation, and is standard.
- **Interference coupling degree.** Hu et al. (ICC 2022) tie the entire coordination mechanism to load-coupled interference. Higher reuse pressure / tighter coupling widens the gap.
- **Partial observability.** InforMARL (Nayak et al., "Scalable Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning through Intelligent Information Aggregation," ICML 2023; arXiv 2211.02127) shows graph aggregation wins precisely when observations are *local*; they also find that giving agents *global* state "may not necessarily improve performance, and can, in fact, overwhelm the RL agent networks and lead to increased sample complexity." If your agents currently see too much, message passing has nothing to add — restricting observation to strictly local + neighbor messages is a legitimate way to create the information asymmetry that graphs exploit.
- **Non-stationarity / time-varying topology, heterogeneity, delayed/noisy observations** are all cited (Liu et al. 2024; the V2X GNN-DRL line) as conditions under which graphs extract global structure and denoise.

Bottom line: the literature's own framing is that GNN-MARL is "primarily about scalability and zero-shot transfer rather than same-size same-topology performance." Your DQN family's 0/20 separations are exactly what this predicts.

### RQ3 — Zero-shot generalization as the primary claim

This is your strongest pivot. InforMARL's headline result is that in testing "it scales well to environments with arbitrary numbers of agents and obstacles," while "in training, InforMARL has better sample efficiency and performance than baseline approaches, despite using less information" — cross-topology transfer is the contribution, not same-size wins. STACCA ("Transformer-Based Scalable MARL for Networked Systems with Long-Range Interactions," arXiv 2511.13103) makes network-generalizable zero-shot transfer to larger unseen topologies its explicit thesis. Cyber-defense and swarm-control papers (e.g., the DGN lineage; Local-Canonicalization Equivariant GNNs, arXiv 2509.14431) state that MLP-based MAPPO/QMIX "cannot" transfer because "rigid input dimensions fail ... during zero-shot transfer to new subnets with varying node counts."

**Fair-comparison setup:** since MLP/central baselines have fixed input dimension, the accepted, legitimate baseline adaptations to compare against are: (i) **retrain** the MLP/central model natively at each target size (upper bound; shows the GNN matches a retrained model with *no* retraining); (ii) **per-agent MLP with parameter sharing** + fixed-size local neighborhood truncation / nearest-k padding (this is the fair "MLP-can-also-be-inductive" control); (iii) **zero-padding / truncation** of inputs (weak baseline, include for completeness). Reporting "GNN transfers zero-shot; shared-parameter MLP with k-NN truncation degrades by X%; fixed-input MLP cannot run at all" is a clean, honest, and venue-appropriate result. Inductive generalization *is* accepted as a sufficient standalone contribution at INFOCOM/GLOBECOM/TNSM/TWC/JSAC (the JSAC 2021 Shen et al. paper and the TWC Air-MPNN paper both foreground size-generalization as the contribution). This experiment was in your original plan and should be promoted to the primary claim.

### RQ4 — Statistical power and seed instability

Your CIs are wide because you are averaging a bimodal distribution. Agarwal et al. ("Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice," NeurIPS 2021, Outstanding Paper; arXiv 2108.13264) warn that "reliable evaluation in the few run deep RL regime cannot ignore the uncertainty in results without running the risk of slowing down progress in the field," and advocate reporting interval estimates and performance profiles with robust aggregates — IQM with stratified-bootstrap CIs and probability-of-improvement via the rliable library. You already use rliable, so add IQM, P(improvement), and performance profiles. But the deeper fix for bimodality is to **stop treating collapse as continuous**: model "does this seed serve cell-edge UEs at all?" as a Bernoulli outcome and report the **collapse rate with a Clopper–Pearson or Wilson binomial CI**. With 5 seeds a collapse rate of 2/5 has a Wilson 95% CI spanning roughly 0.12–0.74 — far too wide to conclude anything, which is exactly your current situation. Colas, Sigaud & Oudeyer ("How Many Random Seeds? Statistical Power Analysis in Deep RL Experiments," arXiv 1806.08295) give the machinery to pick N from the observed effect size and variance; for a bimodal 0/1 outcome with base rate ~20–40%, you need on the order of 20–30 seeds per algorithm to get a usable binomial CI, and they recommend using *more* seeds than the minimum to guard against variance mis-estimation. Henderson et al. ("Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters," AAAI 2018) is the canonical warning that 5 seeds can manufacture spurious "significant" differences purely from seed variance. **Per-seed collapse is itself a legitimate primary finding**: "architecture X collapses in k of n seeds" is a discrete, reportable, decision-relevant result.

### RQ5 — Training stability as a contribution

Yes. Reliability/worst-case metrics are established. Formalize collapse via: (i) **worst-seed performance** (min over seeds of embb_p5_mbps); (ii) **CVaR** at, say, the 20% level (mean of the worst fifth of seed-episodes) — see Du, Wang & Huang ("Provably Efficient Risk-Sensitive RL: Iterated CVaR and Worst Path," arXiv 2206.02678) and Ying et al. ("Towards Safe RL via Constraining Conditional Value-at-Risk / CPPO," arXiv 2206.04436) for CVaR as a robustness objective; (iii) **probability of failure** (the binomial collapse rate from RQ4). rliable's optimality-gap and performance-profile tooling directly support "how often does each method fall below an acceptable cell-edge threshold." If central-ppo and gnn-mappo_sage never collapse while gnn-madqn_gat collapses 2/5, that *is* a finding — though note it currently cuts against GAT-DQN, so honesty requires reporting it whichever way it falls.

### RQ6 — Mechanism-level evidence when KPIs saturate

Accepted, and well-precedented in wireless GNN work: attention is used to "manage interfering relationships between users" (Nature Scientific Reports 2025, "Jumping knowledge graph attention network for resource allocation"), and GAT attention has been analyzed against known coupling matrices (e.g., the electricity-load GNN study, arXiv 2507.03690, which correlates learned attention with a physical coupling matrix and uses PCA of attention vectors to show regime separation). Concrete tests for your paper: (i) correlate GAT edge-attention weights against your true pathloss/interference-coupling matrix (report Pearson/Spearman across episodes); (ii) show emergent behavior — do attending neighbors avoid simultaneous co-slice PRB allocation more than non-attending pairs?; (iii) causal validation via attention ablation. **Crucial caveat you must state:** the interpretability literature (Jain & Wallace-style critiques; Michel et al. 2019; Voita et al. 2019) shows attention weights do *not* always correlate with functional importance, so correlational attention analysis must be backed by a causal ablation (randomize/uniformize attention and show KPI degradation) to count as evidence rather than decoration.

### RQ7 — The overflow-vs-deadline issue

Your drops are dominated by buffer overflow, which means you are measuring a congestion/blocking phenomenon, not a latency-deadline phenomenon — this genuinely undermines URLLC *latency* claims, because a packet dropped at ingress never experienced the queueing delay your deadline is meant to test. URLLC papers model the binding mechanism as a hard deadline: "the latency requirement is a hard deadline, such that the packet ... [is] dropped when the deadline is missed," and reliability is the fraction of deadline misses. Buffers are dimensioned so the queue can hold a deadline's worth of arrivals and the *deadline*, not capacity, is what expires packets; finite-buffer M/M/W and M/G/1/(K,∞) priority-queue treatments (e.g., the bursty-URLLC M/M/W papers, arXiv 1912.00579 and 2001.04161) formalize this. **Rule of thumb:** set `urllc_max_bits ≳ arrival_rate × deadline` (plus a burst margin) so that at the target load the queue almost never overflows before the deadline fires; then deadline-drop dominates and your SLA metric measures what it claims to. This is your preliminary recommendation #3 and it is well-founded.

### RQ8 — Methodological legitimacy / ethics

The line is well defined in the reproducibility literature. **Legitimate:** (a) diagnosing that KPIs saturated and the constraint never binds; (b) redefining the operating point via an *a-priori, model-agnostic* criterion ("the trained baseline's violation must sit near δ"; "utilization must exceed X%"); (c) applying the change identically to all 8 algorithms; (d) preregistering the revised hypotheses, metrics, and load *before* the confirmatory run; (e) reporting both waves. Hildebrandt ("Preregistration of Machine Learning Research Design: Against P-Hacking," 2019) and the ML-reproducibility literature (e.g., "Perspectives on Machine Learning from Psychology's Reproducibility Crisis," arXiv 2104.08878) endorse preregistering the hyperparameter/operating-point search space and referencing it in the paper as a foil against HARKing and selective reporting. **Illegitimate (result-chasing):** choosing the load/δ/buffer values *because* you observed they make the GNN win; sweeping many regimes and reporting only the favorable one; adding capacity headroom only for the GNN; or changing the metric set after seeing results to promote the KPI the GNN happens to lead. The tell-tale distinction: your justification sentence must reference a property of the *task* (binding constraint, contention, locality), never a property of the *outcome* (which model wins).

Reviewer reaction: a paper that says "in regime A (light load) no architecture separates; in regime B (contested load) the GNN separates" is *well-received* if and only if regime B is justified independently and both regimes are reported. Survey work on MARL (Hernandez-Leal et al., "A Survey and Critique of Multiagent Deep Reinforcement Learning," arXiv 1810.05587) explicitly flags "cherry planting" (adapting an environment to a specific algorithm) as a bad practice, while also noting the community "would benefit from reporting negative results for carefully designed hypotheses" — disclosing your criterion up front is what separates the two.

### RQ9 — Publishing the negative result

Concretely publishable. Venues/formats:
- **ICBINB ("I Can't Believe It's Not Better")** — a recurring NeurIPS/ICLR workshop series explicitly for "surprising or negative results" and "empirical falsification"; a 2026 ICLR edition ("Challenges in Applied Deep Learning") is in the workshop pipeline. Your "GNN-MARL does not beat MLP under light load; here is why and when it would" is squarely in scope.
- **Reproducibility / benchmark / empirical-study tracks.** Formanek et al., "Dispelling the Mirage of Progress in Offline MARL through Standardised Baselines and Evaluation" (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks Track; arXiv 2406.09068) is a direct precedent: "on 35 out of 47 datasets used in prior work (almost 75% of cases), we match or surpass the performance of the current purported SOTA ... our baselines often substantially outperform these more sophisticated algorithms." That is the model for framing yours.
- **Networking venues** accept "empirical study / lessons-learned" papers; TNSM and the ORAN/xApp workshops are receptive to calibration and negative-but-informative findings, especially with a clean methodology and released code.
The position paper "Embracing Negative Results in Machine Learning" (arXiv 2406.03980) argues subfields need dedicated space for these; cite it to frame the contribution's value.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Fix the task so the constraint binds (do first; largest legitimate impact).**
1. **Recalibrate `lambda_arrival` against a trained policy.** Train one baseline to convergence, measure held-out violation, raise load until steady-state violation sits near δ (target 5–12% with the constraint occasionally active). *Paper sentence:* "We calibrate offered load against a trained rather than a static policy so that the CMDP constraint is active at the operating point, following the observation that coordination gains in slicing appear only under load-coupled contention (Hu et al., ICC 2022)."
2. **Lower δ** (e.g., from 0.12 toward ~0.05) so the dual variable must actually push. *Sentence:* "δ was set so that a converged policy operates at the constraint boundary, ensuring λ is informative."
3. **Fix buffer dimensioning:** set `urllc_max_bits ≳ arrival_rate × deadline` + burst margin so deadline-drop dominates. *Sentence:* "Buffers are sized to the delay–bandwidth product so that deadline expiry, not ingress overflow, is the binding SLA mechanism, consistent with hard-deadline URLLC models."
4. **Promote `embb_p5_mbps` (cell-edge) to a first-class metric** and add collapse-rate as a discrete outcome.
Apply all four identically to all 8 algorithms. Benchmark that would change the plan: if after Stage 1 the trained violation still sits >5pp below δ, raise load further or add gNB before proceeding.

**Stage 2 — Make architecture matter.**
5. **Scale the topology:** add gNB (10, then 20) and/or tighten interference coupling. *Sentence:* "Because graph inductive bias is a scalability property (Shen et al., JSAC 2021), we evaluate at 5/10/20 gNB." Benchmark: if no separation emerges by 20 gNB under a binding constraint, the same-size claim is genuinely unsupported and you pivot fully to Stages 3–4.
6. **Restrict observations to local + neighbor messages** so message passing has information to add (InforMARL).

**Stage 3 — Promote the honest headline claims.**
7. **Zero-shot transfer** (train 5, test 20/50) with the three baseline adaptations (retrain upper bound; shared-param MLP + k-NN truncation; fixed-input MLP that cannot run). This is likely your strongest paper.
8. **Stability/collapse:** report worst-seed, CVaR@20%, and Wilson-CI collapse rate.
9. **Mechanism:** GAT attention vs. interference matrix, validated by attention-ablation.

**Stage 4 — Statistical rigor.**
10. **Raise seeds to ~20–30** for the bimodal cell-edge outcome; report IQM + stratified-bootstrap CIs + P(improvement) (rliable) for continuous KPIs and binomial CIs for collapse.
11. **Preregister** revised hypotheses, metrics, load, δ, and buffer rule before the confirmatory wave; cite the preregistration in the paper.

**Thresholds that change the recommendation:** if Stage 1 makes KPIs vary but still no CI separation at 20 gNB → the same-size null is real; publish it as an empirical/ICBINB study plus the zero-shot and stability contributions. If zero-shot also fails to separate → fall back to the mechanism + reproducibility paper (below).

## What Would Make This Indefensible (avoid)
- **Tuning load/δ/buffer to the outcome.** Setting any parameter *because* you saw it makes the GNN win. The only admissible justification references task properties (binding constraint, contention, locality), never which model leads.
- **Asymmetric changes.** Giving the GNN more capacity, more training steps, extra observation channels, or a different floor mode than the baselines. Every change must hit all 8 algorithms identically (as you already enforce).
- **Regime-shopping with selective reporting.** Sweeping many loads/topologies and reporting only the favorable one. Report the full grid or preregister the single chosen point.
- **Post-hoc metric promotion to the winner.** Elevating `embb_p5_mbps` *after* seeing the GNN leads there. You can promote it — but justify it a priori as the cell-edge fairness proxy the coordination mechanism is designed to protect, and preregister that.
- **Dropping the collapsing seeds** or re-seeding until the GNN looks stable. Collapse is data; report it.
- **Claiming URLLC latency gains while overflow dominates.** Fix the buffer first or restrict the claim to what the mechanism actually measures.

## Fallback Plan — the strongest honest paper if GNN-MARL is still comparable
If, after a binding-constraint regime, topology scaling to 20 gNB, restricted observations, and 20–30 seeds, GNN-MARL is *still* statistically comparable on same-size KPIs, the strongest honest paper is a **methodology + empirical-study contribution**, not an architecture-superiority paper. Its thesis: *"Under a correctly calibrated, constraint-binding 5G slicing CMDP, graph inductive bias yields no same-size KPI advantage over well-tuned MLP baselines; its value is confined to zero-shot topology generalization and worst-case robustness."* Contributions: (1) a released, calibrated Gymnasium/PettingZoo slicing benchmark with a trained-policy calibration protocol (reusable by the community — this alone has value, cf. the offline-MARL standardization precedent); (2) the load/interference *phase boundary* at which coordination begins to separate (a positive scientific result even if 5 gNB is below it); (3) zero-shot transfer as the one regime where the GNN is categorically superior (MLP cannot run); (4) collapse-rate/CVaR robustness characterization; (5) attention-vs-interference mechanism analysis. Target ICBINB, a reproducibility/benchmark track, or TNSM as an empirical study. This is a defensible, citable, thesis-worthy outcome regardless of which architecture "wins."

## Caveats
- The strongest quantitative "gap grows with density" evidence (Shen et al.) is from *power control*, not slicing, and its canonical version is 2019 (the in-window journal version is JSAC 2021); the slicing evidence (Hu et al. ICC 2022) shows a real but more modest coordination-specific increment than its headline 2× suggests. Treat "graphs help under contention" as well-supported in direction but calibrate expectations on magnitude for slicing specifically.
- Attention-weight interpretability is genuinely contested; correlational attention-vs-interference analysis is not sufficient on its own and must be backed by causal ablation.
- Seed-count guidance (20–30) is an order-of-magnitude target from Colas et al.'s framework applied to a bimodal Bernoulli outcome; you should recompute N from your own observed collapse base rate and desired power once Stage 1 stabilizes the distribution.
- Some sources surfaced are arXiv preprints or non-archival workshop pages; prefer the peer-reviewed versions (JSAC 2021, ICC 2022, TWC 2023, NeurIPS 2021/2024, AAAI 2018) for citation in the thesis.
- Preregistration norms in ML are still maturing and debated for exploratory hyperparameter work; use it as a transparency foil (declare the operating-point criterion and metric set in advance), not as a rigid constraint on legitimate exploration.
